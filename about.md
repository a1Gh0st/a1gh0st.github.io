## The Mission

**A1Gh0st is a pseudonymous cybersecurity portfolio built around hands-on practice, technical curiosity, and clear documentation.**

The goal is simple: learn how systems work, understand how they fail, practice security techniques in authorized environments, and turn those lessons into evidence-based technical work.

This portfolio is intentionally focused on **projects, findings, and demonstrated progress**, rather than the personal identity behind the brand.

## Areas of Focus

- **Penetration testing:** Reconnaissance, enumeration, vulnerability assessment, exploitation in permitted labs, and technical reporting.
- **Networking and operating systems:** Linux, Windows, TCP/IP, web technologies, and system security fundamentals.
- **Web application security:** Understanding requests, authentication, input handling, and common vulnerability classes.
- **Scripting and automation:** Developing practical skills with Python, Bash, JavaScript, and security utilities.
- **Red teaming and AI security:** Longer-term areas of study, built on a strong offensive-security foundation.

These are developing areas of technical focus, not claims of expertise or professional certification.

## Methodology

**Learn. Practice. Document. Improve.**

1. **Learn:** Build a foundation in the technologies and security principles behind a problem.
2. **Practice:** Apply that knowledge in owned or explicitly authorized environments.
3. **Document:** Explain the objective, tools, process, evidence, limitations, and findings.
4. **Improve:** Review mistakes, refine the approach, and carry useful lessons forward.

The priority is accuracy over appearance. A completed exercise is documented as an exercise; a training path is not presented as a paid certification; and conclusions should be supported by observable evidence.

## The Road Ahead

Current work centers on strengthening cybersecurity fundamentals and progressing through TryHackMe training. The longer-term direction is penetration testing, web application security, red-team techniques, and eventually AI security.

As the portfolio grows, each new project should contribute something concrete: a useful script, a tested technique, an investigation, or a well-explained lesson.
